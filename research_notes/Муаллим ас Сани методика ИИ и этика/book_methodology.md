# «Муаллим ас-Сани» (Мөгаллим сәни / Мұғалім сәни / Muallimi soniy / معلم ثاني) Ахмадхади Максуди: история, издания, структура, методика, типичные ошибки

Researched 2026-10-17. Reliability labels: [A] = academic or library or official source; [B] = established media or encyclopedia profile; [C] = publisher, marketplace, blog or forum (low reliability, treat as indicative only).

## 1. History: first publication, purpose, usul-i jadid context, Maqsudi's biography, the companion «Муаллим аль-Авваль», and use today

### Takeaway
Ахмадхади Максуди (1868–1941), a Kazan teacher of Arabic and a Jadid, wrote two primers. «Мөгаллим әүвәл» («Первый учитель», 1892) is a syllable and sound primer for Tatar literacy in Arabic script. «Мөгаллим сәни» («Второй учитель», commonly dated to the mid-1890s; the exact first-edition year is NOT verified) bridges from knowing the Arabic script to reading the Quran correctly with basic tajwid. Both books belong to the Jadid shift from the letter-naming method (буквослагательный) to the sound method (звуковой). The two primers reached about 32 editions in the author's lifetime and more than 1 million copies, and they are still reprinted across Russia, Kazakhstan and Central Asia.

### Cited Findings
**Biography**
- Born 28 Sept 1868 in the village of Tashsu, Kazan uyezd, into the family of a mullah. Studied 1881–1890 at the madrasa of the Apanaev mosque in Kazan, then taught Arabic there. In 1893 Ismail Gasprinsky invited him to teach in Bakhchisaray, where he worked about 3 years. Passed exams at the Kazan Tatar Teachers' School (1896–1899) and audited courses at Kazan University (1903–1905). Published the newspaper «Йолдыз» (Yulduz) 1906–1918. His brother was Sadri Maksudi, a Duma deputy. Arrested in 1917, 1932 (3 years of exile in Vyatka) and 1938; the 1938 case was closed in Jan 1939. Died 28 June 1941. [B] — [БИЗНЕС Online, М. Бирин, «По книгам брата Садри Максуди продолжают изучать арабский язык в странах бывшего СССР» (c. Jan 2022)](https://www.business-gazeta.ru/article/537685)
- Profile: public and political figure, teacher and journalist. Author of «Muallim awwal» («First teacher», 1892), a syllabic primer, and of «Muallim thani» («Second teacher»), a primer for reading and writing Arabic, plus «Durus shifahiya» (1898) and Tatar grammar works (1910). [B] — [Bolgar Islamic Academy profile](https://bolgar.academy/en/ahmed-hadi-maqsoudi/)

**Dating and purpose of the two books**
- «Мугаллим эввель» (1892) added 6 letters to the Arabic script to represent Tatar vowels. It combined the sound and syllable methods with illustrations and was reprinted more than 30 times (over 1 million copies); its ideas underlay the 1920 «яңа имля» orthography. «Мугаллим сани» is dated «середина 1890-х». It bridges from mastering the Arabic script to correct Quran reading (tajwid rules, pronunciation, from simple elements to complex texts) and was used for Tatars, Bashkirs, Kazakhs, Uzbeks and Kyrgyz. [B] — [Ф. Калимуллина (к.и.н., Институт татарской энциклопедии АН РТ), Milliard.Tatar, 30.07.2026](https://milliard.tatar/news/axmetxadi-maksudi-tatarin-naucivsii-aziyu-arabice-tadzvidu-i-sozdavsii-bestseller-po-verouceniyu-islama-10101)
- «Муаллим авваль» first appeared in 1892. Both books are syllable-based Arabic primers; together they reached 32 editions in the author's lifetime, with more than 1 million copies (about 1.2 million) printed by the early 20th century. **Usul-i jadid context**
- The core Jadid reform was the move from the traditional буквослагательный method to the звуковой method. This cut the time needed to learn to read «с нескольких лет до одного учебного года». In old-method mektebs, learners first memorised the letter names (алиф, ба, та, са…); in the sound method each letter is linked directly to its sound. Gasprinsky first systematised the method in «Ходжа-и сыбьян» (Бахчисарай, 1892). [A] — [Дамиров Р.В., «К вопросу о творческом наследии шейха З. Расулева: на основе труда «Әлифба хакында»», Minbar. Islamic Studies 2026;19(2):299–312, DOI 10.31162/2618-9569-2026-19-2-299-312](https://www.minbar.su/jour/article/download/1664/836), citing Гумеров М.М., Minbar 2022;15(4):856–868.
- Old method (described for Central Asia): pupils first named the letters of a syllable, and only then read the syllable. [A/B] — [ojs.rmasav.com](https://ojs.rmasav.com/index.php/ojs/article/download/770/nARTC_2_1_B_30/759) (snippet only)
- Dамиров also shows that Z. Rasulev (Orenburg, 1912) justified the sound method («саутиййа») through tajwid tradition (Мулла Али аль-Кари) rather than European pedagogy. This is useful framing for a Quran-oriented method. [A] — [Minbar 2026](https://www.minbar.su/jour/article/download/1664/836)

**Use today**
- The books are still reprinted in large runs and sold in most bookshops in Central Asia, European Russia, the Caucasus and Dagestan; an Uzbek blogger is quoted as saying that Uzbek children learn to read from them. [B] — [БИЗНЕС Online](https://www.business-gazeta.ru/article/537685)
- Kazakhstan: in 2014 the Spiritual Administration of Muslims of Kazakhstan (ДУМК), under Chief Mufti Ержан Малғажыұлы, launched a project to distribute «Мұғалім сәни» to mosques and madrasas. Distribution began at the Karaganda regional central mosque. [A, official] — [muftyat.kz, 01.02.2014](https://www.muftyat.kz/kk/news/country/2014-02-01/526-mugalim-sni-kitabyn-taratu-zhobasy/)
- In early-20th-century Kazakhstan, teachers in Jadid madrasas adapted Maksudi's method to Kazakh pronunciation; in Uzbekistan, his primers were base textbooks in areas under Tatar influence. New editions are issued in Tatarstan for madrasas and for home study. [B] — [Milliard.Tatar 2026](https://milliard.tatar/news/axmetxadi-maksudi-tatarin-naucivsii-aziyu-arabice-tadzvidu-i-sozdavsii-bestseller-po-verouceniyu-islama-10101)

### Inferences
- The best-supported description for the thesis: «Мөгаллим әүвәл» (1892) is the Tatar literacy primer, and «Мөгаллим сәни» (1890s; the widely reprinted text descends from Kazan editions of c. 1917–1918) is the Arabic and Quran reading primer. The two are often confused online.
- The book is used across Russian Tatarstan, Kazakhstan and Central Asia, and Kazakhstan's ДУМК endorsed it at an official level (2014). This supports choosing it as the base text for a bot aimed at Russian- and Kazakh-speaking learners.

## 3. Lesson structure (order of topics, number of lessons, exercises)

### Takeaway
Two independent sources agree on how the book opens: the letters are not introduced in alphabetical order. Instead they come in small groups, starting with ا ر ز م (then ت, ن ي ب ك ل و, etc.), each with vowelled syllables and words. After all letters are covered, the book moves to madd letters, tashdid, tanwin, hamza, ta marbuta, then tajwid (nun or mim with sukun, lam rules, madd types), waqf signs, and finally reading short surahs. Exercise style: lines of vowelled syllables or words to be read aloud, then short Quranic passages. The researcher found no verified table of contents of the original book.

### Inferences
- The grouped-letter sequence starting with ا ر ز م is pedagogically motivated. These are non-connecting or simple shapes, and the group lets learners build real syllables immediately (e.g., زَرَ، مَرَ). Introducing all three short vowels (fatha, kasra, damma) together with the first group is consistent with the sound method (no letter-name memorisation). A bot can mirror this order: Unit 1 = ا ر ز م + harakat; Units 2–5 = remaining letter groups; Unit 6 = madd and shadda; Unit 7 = tanwin, hamza, ة; Units 8–12 = nun or mim sakina, lam, madd types; Unit 13 = waqf; Unit 14 = surahs.
- Lesson boundaries vary by edition and course (16 vs 18), so the bot's design should cite one specific edition.

## 4. Teaching methodology in practice (talaqqi, syllable reading, duration, progress checks)

### Takeaway
Sources consistently describe the method as sound plus syllable (звуко-слоговой): the teacher pronounces and the learner repeats, letter groups are read immediately as vowelled syllables, then words, then Quranic text. Learning to read under the Jadid method took about one school year, against years in old-method schools; contemporaries said pupils read simple words within a few lessons. No published syllabus with hours or formal progress criteria for «Муаллим сани» courses in Russia or Kazakhstan was found.

### Inferences
- The method the bot can operationalise is: (1) hear the model (audio of a teacher or qari); (2) repeat (the learner records a voice message); (3) read the syllable or word line independently; (4) the teacher (here, the bot plus a human check) corrects; (5) move on only when the line is read without error. This is a digital approximation of talaqqi or mushafaha. Classical tradition treats it as not fully replaceable, which the book's own disclaimer echoes and which is relevant to the thesis's ethics section.
- Progress criteria in practice appear to be the teacher's judgement of error-free oral reading of the lesson's lines; no standardised rubric was found.

## 5. Typical errors of Russian- and Turkic-speaking beginners

### Takeaway
The hardest areas for Russian (and largely Kazakh/Tatar) speakers are: pharyngeals ع and ح (ح → Russian «х», ع → glottal stop or vowel); uvular ق → «к» and غ/خ confusion; interdentals ث → с/т, ذ → з/д, ظ → з; emphatics ص ض ط ظ pronounced as plain с д т з; vowel length (madd) shortened; shadda not doubled; and ر softened before front vowels. The researcher found no peer-reviewed study specifically on Russian- or Kazakh-speaking learners of Quranic reading. Evidence comes from methodological sites plus studies of other L1 groups (Indonesian, Hausa) showing the same set of hard phonemes.

### Cited Findings
- Russian speakers: ع has no Russian counterpart and is confused with ء or pronounced as a vowel. ح and خ are both reduced to Russian «х» and confused with each other (meaning-changing). ث → «с»/«т», ذ → «з»/«д». ق → «к» (too far forward) or replaced by غ. Emphatics ص ض ط ظ are produced without pharyngealisation and confused with each other. Long vowels are shortened. Shadda is pronounced single. ر is softened before front vowels. [C, author not given] — [lingust.ru FAQ](https://lingust.ru/faq/arabic/1958-7)
- Replacing an emphatic with a plain consonant changes meaning, e.g., [tīn] «инжир» vs [ṭīn] «глина». [B] — [Викиучебник «Арабский язык/Фонетика»](https://ru.wikibooks.org/wiki/%D0%90%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D1%8F%D0%B7%D1%8B%D0%BA/%D0%A4%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B0)
- Аббясов's 2005 Quran-reading manual for Russian-speaking madrasa students (Совет муфтиев России) organises pronunciation teaching around organs of speech, makharij, heavy vs light letters, and the treatment of ل and ر. It notes, for example, that under certain conditions a sound «близкий к русскому гласному «Э»» is heard (imala context). This is useful for contrastive Russian–Arabic explanations. [A-] — [PDF](https://mahalla1.ru/documents/Muallim-Sani.pdf)
- Historically, a central methodological problem for Jadid reformers was teaching Arabic sounds absent from Turkic phonology. Rasulev (1912) adapted the description of sifat (sound features) to the phonetic features of Turkic languages. [A] — [Дамиров, Minbar 2026](https://www.minbar.su/jour/article/download/1664/836)
- Kazakh-speaking learners: the tutor advises extra attention to makharij because Kazakh lacks some Arabic sounds. [C] — [arabsha.wordpress.com](https://arabsha.wordpress.com/tag/муалим-сани/)
- Cross-linguistic evidence: Indonesian students made the most errors on ع and ض, with frequent errors on ح ص ع and on ض ط ق (Lathifah et al., *Arabiyat* 2017). [A] — [Arabiyat / DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/7061d63bacf94a918fa406c86c37b399). Hausa speakers find ث ح خ ع ص ض ذ ظ hardest because these are absent from L1. [A] — [Journal of Language Studies](https://jls.tu.edu.iq/index.php/JLS/article/view/371). For non-Arab learners generally, pharyngeal, alveo-dental and interdental sounds are the hardest (Alsulaiman et al., 2014). [A] — [Ulster University Pure](https://pure.ulster.ac.uk/en/publications/pronunciation-errors-of-non-arab-learners-of-arabic-language/)

### Inferences
- Contrastive prediction for Kazakh speakers (not empirically verified; derived from Kazakh phonology, which has uvular [q] and [ʁ] as allophones of к and г next to back vowels): Kazakh speakers may find ق and غ easier than Russian speakers, but they share the difficulties with ع, ح, the interdentals and the emphatics. Kazakh vowel harmony and the absence of phonemic vowel length make madd errors likely. This should be flagged as a hypothesis in the thesis.
- For the bot, the priority set of minimal-pair drills is: ح/ه/خ, ع/ء, ص/س, ض/د, ط/ت, ظ/ذ/ز, ث/س, ق/ك, غ/خ, short vs long vowels, single vs geminate consonants.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that specifically quantifies Arabic pronunciation errors of Russian-speaking or Kazakh-speaking Quran learners (KiberLeninka results mostly concern Arabs learning Russian, e.g., [«Типичные ошибки студентов-арабов при изучении фонетики русского языка»](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/tipichnye-oshibki-studentov-arabov-pri-izuchenii-fonetiki-russkogo-yazyka-kak-inostrannogo)). Recommend searching eLibrary for «постановка арабского произношения русскоязычным студентам» and the Вестник КазНУ, серия востоковедения, directly.
