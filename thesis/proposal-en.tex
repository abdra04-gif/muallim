\section{Introduction and research problem}

People all over the world learn to read Arabic to access the Quran in its original language. One method
popular in Russia and Kazakhstan is \emph{al-Muallim ath-Thani} (``The Second Teacher'') \cite{SRC-040}: it
introduces beginners to the letters gradually, one by one, building on Quranic vocabulary. Learning usually
requires a human instructor, yet progress depends heavily on how much the learner practises between classes.
We aim to support this out-of-class practice with an AI assistant that checks learners' reading and detects
mistakes.

Several solutions already check Quranic reading. Commercial apps such as Tarteel highlight mistakes in
recitation \cite{SRC-046}, but they are closed, so their accuracy on beginners cannot be verified. Open models
such as Quran Muaalem recognise phonemes, yet they are trained on expert reciters and their quality depends on
the input speech: the phoneme error rate (PER) is 0.54\,\% on the authors' test set and 5.78\,\% on
qdat\_bench, which contains real recitation errors \cite{SRC-002}. Whether these solutions are adequate for
adult beginners reading primer material is unknown; checking this is part of our study (RQ-1).

\textbf{Problem.} Not everyone can study with a teacher often: work, distance and the cost of lessons get in
the way, and a group class has no time to go through each learner's difficulties. But existing open datasets, including the recitations of
non-Arabic speakers in RetaSy \cite{SRC-032}, do not provide data for assessing the reading of Russian-speaking
adult beginners learning from primer material such as \emph{al-Muallim ath-Thani}, with detailed annotation
of reading and pronunciation errors. Without such data the quality of existing models on these learners can
be neither measured nor improved.

\section{Research questions}

\textbf{Hypothesis.} By collecting a small curated speech dataset, existing solutions can be evaluated, and
agentic loop-engineering can be used to develop an effective tool for beginners' out-of-class practice.

\begin{rqbox}
\textbf{RQ-1.} How reliably do existing speech recognition and analysis models detect reading and pronunciation errors in beginners learning Arabic and Quran reading?\\[4pt]
\textbf{RQ-2.} To what extent does applying agentic loop-engineering to search for and combine configurations of existing models and components improve the accuracy of reading and pronunciation error detection compared with baseline approaches?\\[4pt]
\textbf{RQ-3 (supplementary).} To what extent does using the developed tool in out-of-class practice affect how beginners studying with a teacher progress in mastering the course material?
\end{rqbox}

\textbf{Method.} A Telegram bot collects recordings with participants' consent. Pilot: 20--40 adults,
4--6 weeks. Two teachers annotate the recordings by phoneme and error type: a \emph{reading error} means the
wrong sign was read (another vowel, a missed sukun, shadda or madd, a look-alike letter); a
\emph{pronunciation error} means the sign was read correctly but the sound was produced wrongly.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Baselines:} Quran Muaalem as is, and the best standalone off-the-shelf model.
  \item \emph{Agentic loop-engineering:} an AI agent searches for and combines configurations of off-the-shelf
    models and components, for example a verifier and adaptation of individual models on teacher-annotated
    data (such as LoRA fine-tuning \cite{SRC-111}). Tuning
    uses training learners only, and every configuration tried is recorded in a log \cite{SRC-067}.
  \item \emph{Evaluation:} the best configuration is compared once with the baselines on a frozen test set.
  \item \emph{For RQ-3:} a knowledge tracing model \cite{SRC-124} and a teacher test at three time points.
\end{itemize}

\section{Novelty and contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A curated speech dataset of adult beginners learning Arabic reading as a foreign language, at early
    stages of primer-based learning, with detailed expert annotation separating reading errors from
    pronunciation errors.
  \item Measured reliability of off-the-shelf models on beginners against teachers' assessment.
  \item An error detection system assembled with agentic loop-engineering: its gain over the baselines and a
    log of all configurations.
  \item Supplementary: how learners progress through the material when using the tool.
\end{enumerate}

\section{State of the art}

Quran Muaalem recognises phonemes and articulatory attributes \cite{SRC-002}. The QuranMB and
IQRA 2026 benchmarks address mispronunciation detection \cite{SRC-004,SRC-031}. The closest corpus of
non-native readers, RetaSy, holds about 7,000 recordings from more than 11 countries; it records the reader's
country but not the native language \cite{SRC-032}. None of these corpora separates reading errors from
pronunciation errors. Automatic pronunciation feedback has a medium positive effect ($g=0.69$)
\cite{SRC-025}, but on accented speech it produces false alarms, so the teacher stays in the loop
\cite{SRC-026}. Tutors that listen to reading aloud exist for children \cite{SRC-130}. Knowledge
tracing estimates skill mastery from a sequence of attempts \cite{SRC-124} and has been applied to reading
skills from speech recogniser output \cite{SRC-125}. For adult beginners learning Arabic reading as a foreign
language, the reliability of existing models requires separate evaluation.

\section{Goal of the thesis}

Collect a small curated speech dataset of beginners' primer reading, annotated by teachers, and evaluate
existing models on it. Use agentic loop-engineering to build a reading and pronunciation error detection
system from these models for out-of-class practice, and measure how close it comes to the teacher.
