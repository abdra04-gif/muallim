\section{Introduction and research problem}

In Russia and Kazakhstan, the ``Second Teacher'' (\emph{al-Muallim ath-Thani}) method of teaching Arabic
reading is popular: beginners use it to learn to read the Quran from scratch \cite{SRC-040}.
Independent practice needs automatic reading assessment, but open Quran recitation recognition models are
trained on expert reciters. On recitations with errors their accuracy drops: the phoneme error rate rises from
0.54 to 5.78\,\% \cite{SRC-002}. These models have not been measured on beginners learning Arabic as a
foreign language.

\textbf{Problem.} Not everyone can study with a teacher: work, distance and the cost of lessons get in the
way, and a group class has no time to go through each learner's difficulties. Automatic assessment could
serve as individual practice between lessons. But there is no data on the reading of beginners learning
Arabic as a foreign language, so the quality of existing models on such learners can be neither measured nor
improved.

\section{Research questions}

\textbf{Hypothesis.} By collecting a small curated speech dataset, existing solutions can be evaluated, and
agentic loop-engineering can be used to develop an effective tool for beginners' out-of-class practice.

\begin{rqbox}
\textbf{RQ-1.} How reliably do existing speech recognition and analysis models detect reading and pronunciation errors in beginners learning Arabic and Quran reading?\\[4pt]
\textbf{RQ-2.} To what extent does applying agentic loop-engineering to select and combine existing models and components improve the accuracy of reading and pronunciation error detection compared with baseline approaches?\\[4pt]
\textbf{RQ-3 (supplementary).} To what extent does using the developed tool in out-of-class practice affect how beginners studying with a teacher progress in mastering the course material?
\end{rqbox}

\textbf{Method.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Bot.} Build a Telegram bot based on the lessons of \emph{The Second Teacher}
    (\emph{al-Muallim ath-Thani}) \cite{SRC-040}. The learner sends a voice message, and the bot checks the
    reading and explains the error.
  \item \textbf{Data collection.} Launch the bot and collect learners' recordings with their consent
    (pilot: 20--40 adults, 4--6 weeks).
  \item \textbf{Annotation.} Two teachers mark reading and pronunciation errors in the recordings.
  \item \textbf{Training.} Fine-tune models on our dataset \cite{SRC-111} and use an AI agent (agentic
    loop-engineering) to combine them into a system that detects errors more accurately \cite{SRC-067}. The
    system is compared once with the baselines (Quran Muaalem as is and the best standalone model) on a frozen
    test set.
  \item \textbf{Effect on learners (additional).} Check whether the bot helps learners master the material
    faster: a knowledge tracing model \cite{SRC-124} and a teacher test at three time points.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Novelty and contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A dataset of beginners' primer reading that separates reading errors from pronunciation errors.
  \item Measured reliability of off-the-shelf models on beginners against teachers' assessment.
  \item An error detection system assembled with agentic loop-engineering: its gain over the baselines and a
    log of all configurations.
  \item Supplementary: how learners progress through the material when using the tool.
\end{enumerate}

\section{State of the art}

Quran Muaalem recognises phonemes and articulatory attributes \cite{SRC-002}. The QuranMB and
IQRA 2026 benchmarks contain fluent recitation \cite{SRC-004,SRC-031}; the closest corpus of
non-native readers, RetaSy, has no Russian speakers \cite{SRC-032}, and no corpus separates reading errors from
pronunciation errors. Automatic pronunciation feedback has a medium positive effect ($g=0.69$)
\cite{SRC-025}, but on accented speech it produces false alarms, so the teacher stays in the loop
\cite{SRC-026}. Tutors that listen to reading aloud exist for children \cite{SRC-125}. Knowledge
tracing estimates skill mastery from attempts \cite{SRC-124}, but recogniser errors have not been measured
against teachers \cite{SRC-125}. No study has measured these models on beginners learning Arabic as a foreign
language.

\section{Goal of the thesis}

Collect a small curated speech dataset of beginners' primer reading, annotated by teachers, and evaluate
existing models on it. Use agentic loop-engineering to build a reading and pronunciation error detection
system from these models for out-of-class practice, and measure how close it comes to the teacher.
