\chapter{Methodology}\label{ch:method}

This chapter explains how the thesis produces and evaluates its artefacts. Section~\ref{sec:method-dsr}
introduces the research paradigm. Sections~\ref{sec:method-req}--\ref{sec:method-gqm} describe how
requirements, architecture and metrics were derived. Sections~\ref{sec:method-hyp}--\ref{sec:method-s2}
present the hypothesis and the two studies, and Sections~\ref{sec:method-prov}--\ref{sec:method-validity} cover
preregistration, ethics and threats to validity. Section~\ref{sec:method-harness} describes how the
development harness protects both the code and the experiments.

\section{Research paradigm}\label{sec:method-dsr}

The thesis follows design science research, which produces knowledge by building an artefact that
addresses a real problem and evaluating it rigorously \cite{SRC-100}. Wieringa separates two kinds of
work inside such a project: \emph{design problems}, which ask how to change the world (``build a tutor
that \ldots''), and \emph{knowledge questions}, which ask what is true about it (``how often does the model
reject correct reading?'', ``how much does agentic pipeline optimization improve accuracy?'') \cite{SRC-101}. The
research questions of this thesis are knowledge questions; the objectives O1--O3, O5 and O6 are design problems
that make them answerable, and O4 and O7 are knowledge problems. Table~\ref{tab:hevner} shows
how the thesis addresses Hevner's seven guidelines.

\section{Requirements}\label{sec:method-req}

\subsection{The audience as the main constraint}

One constraint was stated first and checked against every later decision: the learners are
Russian-speaking adults who learn to read Arabic from zero. Seven rules follow from it: explain every sign
in Russian before it is needed; follow the book's order in small steps; let the learner hear before
reading; explain errors through familiar sounds and show them in the text; consolidate with short
questions; never punish; and treat the Qur'anic text with respect, using the exact text of the Medina
mushaf and starting with \emph{isti'adha} and \emph{basmala}.

\subsection{Functional requirements and user stories}

Requirements were collected from the author's requests, the prototype, the book and the literature, and
written with stable identifiers and MoSCoW priorities. The current specification has 47 functional
requirements, grouped as follows: curriculum from the book (FR-01, FR-16, FR-36--FR-41), assessment and
feedback (FR-03--FR-05, FR-20--FR-22, FR-43), learner experience (FR-02, FR-07, FR-31--FR-35), research
data (FR-06, FR-11--FR-14, FR-17, FR-24, FR-26--FR-29), privacy (FR-08--FR-10, FR-44) and
later interface additions (FR-45--FR-47). User stories
describe the same requirements from the viewpoints of learner, teacher and researcher. A traceability matrix
links each requirement to code and tests; an automated report shows which requirements are covered by
passing tests (59 of 60 at the time of writing).

\section{Architecture design method}\label{sec:method-add}

The architecture was derived with a lightweight form of Attribute-Driven Design \cite{SRC-047}: each
iteration takes one goal and a few drivers (scenarios, requirements, constraints), records decisions as
architecture decision records (ADRs) \cite{SRC-010}, and closes with a test that confirms the decision.
For a single author full iteration documents would be bureaucracy, so an iteration is one line in a table
and the details live in the ADRs, including rejected options. Seven iterations were completed: replaceable
model, database and messenger; research data with personal data kept apart; trustworthy and contestable
assessment; curriculum strictly from the book; an interface usable without a teacher; agent development
that cannot be gamed; and diagnostics without leaking personal data. Chapter~\ref{ch:impl} describes the
results.

\section{Measurement framework}\label{sec:method-gqm}

Metrics were derived with the Goal--Question--Metric method \cite{SRC-048}: a goal is stated in a fixed
template (analyse \emph{object} for the purpose of \emph{purpose} with respect to \emph{quality focus} from
the viewpoint of \emph{stakeholder} in the context of \emph{context}), refined into questions, and only then
into metrics and data. A metric without a goal is not collected. The goals are: G1 annotation
reliability and agreement of off-the-shelf models with teachers (RQ-1), G7 agentic pipeline optimization against baseline configurations (RQ-2), and the descriptive goals G3 explanations (exploratory), G4 engagement and the
beginner's path, G5 system quality, and G6 development with AI agents (Section~\ref{sec:method-harness}).

For the pilot, one \emph{main metric} and one \emph{counter-metric} were chosen. The main metric is the
number of book lines passed by learners for the first time in a week: it grows only when learners move
through the book. Because it can be inflated by lowering the pass threshold, the counter-metric is the
share of falsely accepted attempts, attempts passed by the tutor but not by the teacher's transcription.

\section{Hypotheses}\label{sec:method-hyp}

\textbf{Main hypothesis:} a small curated speech dataset of beginners, annotated by teachers, is enough to show where existing models fail on these learners and, through model adaptation and agentic pipeline optimization, to build a more accurate error-detection tool for out-of-class practice. It is tested by two questions (Section~\ref{sec:rq}): RQ-1 --- ``evaluate existing solutions separately for reading and pronunciation errors'', RQ-2 --- ``under a fixed budget, improve detection by agentic pipeline optimization''.

\section{Participants and materials}\label{sec:method-participants}

The pilot is closed: learners are invited through teachers and the author's acquaintances, and the target is
20--40 adult learners over 4--6 weeks. Participation in research requires a separate consent; a short entry
questionnaire, asked only of consenting learners, records age (18+), first language and prior experience of
reading Arabic. Consent to voice recording explicitly covers training the model on the recording and
releasing the weights. Learners who do not consent can still use the tutor; their attempts are graded but no audio
is kept and they are excluded from the analysis. Learners are split into training, validation and test parts without overlap; test learners are not used for training or tuning. The materials are the cards of the letter and vowel lessons
(pages 10--24 of the book) and, for exploratory analysis, later lessons that learners reach. The unit of
analysis is an attempt; learners are clusters, because attempts by one learner are not independent.

Exclusions are fixed in advance: attempts graded by the development stub; recordings labelled unusable or
off-task by an annotator (counted separately); and attempts before the preregistration freeze.

\section{What we check}\label{sec:method-scope}

Recitation errors were classified long before speech models. The classical tradition separates a
\emph{clear error} (lahn jali: another letter or vowel, an extra or missing letter) from a \emph{hidden error}
(lahn khafi: the letter is recognisable but its attribute or a tajweed rule is broken) \cite{SRC-153}. In an
empirical study of students' recitation, errors of makhraj and letter attributes (sifat) formed the largest
group, and qalqala was the most frequent error within it \cite{SRC-152}. Speech benchmarks either leave tajweed
out \cite{SRC-004} or encode all of it \cite{SRC-002}. \emph{Al-Muallim ath-Thani} starts with letters and
vowel signs and later teaches the basics of tajweed (lessons 54--93), so the thesis needs a boundary: what
exactly is checked and how it is counted.

\paragraph{Scope} We listed everything the book teaches and, before data collection, split it into three parts
(Table~\ref{tab:scope}). The split is recorded in the preregistration and does not change after the data are
seen.

\paragraph{Why part of the rules goes into a separate report} The thesis does not discard the third group: its
rules are annotated and counted but do not enter the main RQ-1 and RQ-2 figures. There are three reasons. First,
the pilot lasts 4--6 weeks and learners will probably reach only lessons 30--53; examples of the rules from
lessons 54--69 will be few, and the main metric would be noisy. Second, these rules are the hardest for the model
itself: Quran Muaalem scores an F1 of about 0.45 on ikhfa against 0.95 on qalqala \cite{SRC-002}, and pooling
classes of such different difficulty would hide where the model fails on beginners. Third, the main metrics stay
comparable with benchmarks that do not assess tajweed \cite{SRC-004,SRC-031}. If enough data for a rule
accumulate by the end of the pilot, it moves into the core by a new preregistration version before the test set
is opened \pending{threshold on the number of examples per rule --- to be approved}.

\paragraph{Two axes of an error} Classifications split errors along two independent axes. The first is the
\emph{cause}: a \emph{reading error} (the wrong sign was read) or a \emph{pronunciation error} (the sign was
right, but the sound came from the wrong place or with the wrong attribute \cite{SRC-059}). The second is the
\emph{consequence}: a clear or a hidden error. The axes cross: \ar{ح} pronounced like \ar{ه} is a pronunciation
error by cause and a clear one by consequence; kasra instead of fatha is a reading error and clear; losing the
ghunna in ikhfa is a pronunciation error and hidden. The cause is not always audible from one sound (\ar{ق} read
as \ar{ك} may be either), so the annotation records three things: \emph{what was heard} (substitution, deletion
or insertion of a phoneme, a wrong attribute; the format follows L2-ARCTIC \cite{SRC-154}); \emph{clear or
hidden} (derived from what was heard: the phoneme changed --- clear, only the attribute changed --- hidden); and
the \emph{cause}, which the teacher sets or marks ``unclear''. RQ-1 (a/b) answers by the teachers' labels, and
the comparison with benchmarks answers by what was heard.

\paragraph{What is still open} Four questions stay open and are marked in the text until the supervisor and a
tajweed teacher confirm them: which sifat belong to the core (only qalqala and tafkhim/tarqiq, or also, for
example, hams and jahr) \pending{OQ-17}; whether shortening or lengthening a natural madd is a clear or a hidden
error \pending{OQ-18}; what to do about qalqala at a stop, which the tutor currently does not count as a
beginner's error although the core checks it \pending{OQ-16}; and whether the Arabic examples above are correct
\pending{OQ-21, to be checked with a teacher}.

\section{Study 1: reliability of off-the-shelf models (RQ-1)}\label{sec:method-s1}

\paragraph{Annotation} Two teachers who read confidently with tajwid annotate every attempt. Each sees the
line of the book, the recording, the canonical phonemes, the phonemes the tutor heard and the errors it
reported. If the tutor heard correctly, the annotator marks \emph{agree}; otherwise \emph{fix} and enters the
phonemes that were actually pronounced. Two further marks are possible: \emph{audio unusable} and
\emph{off task}. A separate flag marks a \emph{self-correction}, when the learner made an error and immediately
re-read correctly in the same message; following the taxonomy of Al Mdfaa et al.\ \cite{SRC-068}, these
attempts are reported in their own block and excluded from the main RQ-1 figures. Annotation can be done in
Telegram or in a spreadsheet package; both write to the same table with the annotator code and the version
of the annotation guide. Annotators see the tutor's answer; this speeds up work but may bias them towards
agreement, which is treated as a threat to validity.

\paragraph{Error type} For each error the annotator gives a type. A \emph{reading error}: the wrong sign was
read --- another vowel, a missed sukun, shadda or madd, look-alike letters (\ar{ب}, \ar{ت} and \ar{ث}). A
\emph{pronunciation error}: the sign was read correctly but the sound was produced wrongly (place of
articulation or attribute). Unclear cases are labelled ``unclear''. Both teachers give the type on all test
attempts and on every tenth training attempt; reliability is Cohen's $\kappa$ with ``unclear'' as its own
category and, as a sensitivity analysis, without it (H1.1). The model's error type is derived by a rule from
the alignment: substitution of a vowel, a sign or a look-alike letter is a reading error, substitution by place
or attribute is a pronunciation error; its agreement with teachers is reported descriptively.

\paragraph{Phoneme-level metrics} For each attempt the canonical, annotated and recognised phoneme sequences
are aligned, and each canonical phoneme is classified as TA, TR, FA or FR (Section~\ref{sec:lr-mdd}).
Precision, recall, F1 and diagnosis accuracy are computed over all phonemes, by error type and by letter
\cite{SRC-064}.

\paragraph{Verdict-level metrics} The teacher's verdict is obtained by applying the same grading policy to
the teacher's transcription, with the thresholds stored in the attempt. FAR is the share of attempts the
tutor passed although the teacher's transcription fails; FRR is the share it failed although the
transcription passes. Agreement is measured with Cohen's $\kappa$ \cite{SRC-104},
\begin{equation}
  \kappa = \frac{p_o - p_e}{1 - p_e},
\end{equation}
where $p_o$ is observed and $p_e$ chance agreement, computed between the tutor and each teacher and between
the two teachers. The teacher--teacher value is the ceiling against which the tutor is judged (H1.2).
Agreement between annotators on individual phonemes is measured with Krippendorff's $\alpha$ for nominal
data, which handles missing labels and any number of coders \cite{SRC-065}.

\paragraph{Uncertainty} 95\,\% confidence intervals are obtained by a cluster bootstrap that resamples
learners with replacement. Comparisons with published benchmarks are qualitative only, because the data
differ.

\paragraph{Error profile} Error frequencies by type, confusion matrices ``expected $\to$ heard'' and
difficulty by book line and by the book's minimal pairs are computed twice: over all errors reported by the
tutor and over errors confirmed by annotators (true rejections). The difference between the two shows how
much of an apparent learner profile is produced by the model. To avoid conclusions driven by one active
learner, every frequency is reported with the number of learners who contribute to it. The confirmed error
profile feeds Study~4.

\section{Study 2: agentic pipeline optimization (RQ-2)}\label{sec:method-s2}

\paragraph{Baselines and the agentic optimization} The \textbf{baseline configurations} are \textbf{B0}, Muaalem as is with
alignment and thresholds (Chapter~\ref{ch:impl}), and the best single off-the-shelf model from Study~1
\pending{list of off-the-shelf models and baselines --- to be approved}. In \textbf{agentic pipeline optimization} an AI
agent proposes a configuration (which models to take, how to combine them, which thresholds and alignment rules
to apply, which components to include), runs it and compares it with teacher annotation by learner-level
cross-validation on the training part; every configuration tried and its metric are logged. The budget
(number of iterations or compute hours) is fixed in the preregistration. Two components the agent can choose
from are prepared in advance.

\paragraph{Components} \textbf{B1} is a verifier on top of B0's outputs.
For every canonical phoneme $j$ of an attempt it receives a feature vector $x_j$: the model's confidence for
the phoneme, the probabilities of the articulatory attributes, the Goodness of Pronunciation score
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{GOP}(p_j) = \frac{1}{d_j}\log\frac{P(p_j\mid O_j)}{\max_{q} P(q\mid O_j)},
\end{equation}
where $O_j$ are the audio frames assigned to the phoneme and $d_j$ their number \cite{SRC-115}, as well as
duration, the error kind from the alignment and the material type (Qur'anic or not). A logistic model or
gradient boosting estimates $\hat p_j = P(\text{error}\mid x_j)$; probabilities are calibrated on separate
learners of the training part, and calibration quality is measured by the expected calibration error (ECE)
\cite{SRC-112}. An attempt is rejected if $\max_j \hat p_j \ge \lambda$. The threshold $\lambda$ is chosen by
conformal risk control \cite{SRC-114}: on the calibration learners, the smallest $\lambda$ is taken for which
\begin{equation}
  \frac{n}{n+1}\,\widehat{\mathrm{FRR}}_n(\lambda) + \frac{1}{n+1} \le \varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{\mathrm{FRR}}_n$ is the false-rejection rate on $n$ calibration attempts and
$\varepsilon=0.05$ (QAS-02). Attempts with $\hat p_j$ in an uncertainty band around the threshold receive a
``not sure'' outcome \cite{SRC-117}: the learner is asked to read again and the attempt goes to a teacher.
The conformal guarantee requires exchangeability; attempts by one learner are dependent, so calibration is
done by learner, and whether the guarantee holds on the test set is checked rather than assumed.

\textbf{B2} is Muaalem fine-tuned with LoRA \cite{SRC-111}: a low-rank correction is added to the attention
matrices $W_0\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}$,
\begin{equation}
  W = W_0 + \tfrac{\alpha}{r}\,BA, \qquad B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r},\; A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times k},\; r\ll\min(d,k),
\end{equation}
and only $A$, $B$ and the output CTC heads are trained, while $W_0$ stays frozen. The training objective is
Muaalem's multi-level CTC on teacher-corrected phonemes. The training data are augmented in two ways: from
the book's minimal pairs (a correct reading of a card with one letter of a pair, labelled with the canonical
phonemes of the paired card, is a substitution example) \cite{SRC-119}, and by speed perturbation
$0.9/1.0/1.1$ \cite{SRC-120}. The hyperparameter grid ($r\in\{8,16\}$, learning rate, number of epochs with
early stopping) is fixed in the preregistration.

\paragraph{Split and test-set protection} Before the agentic optimization first runs, about 30\,\% of learners, stratified
by first language and activity, are assigned to the test set, and the list is fixed with a SHA-256
fingerprint. The split is by learner, not by attempt; otherwise one speaker's voice would appear in both
training and evaluation \cite{SRC-122}. The agent searches configurations --- including B2's
hyperparameters and B1's calibration --- only by 5-fold learner-grouped cross-validation inside the training
part \cite{SRC-121}; the log of every configuration tried is published, so that choosing the best cannot be
mistaken for overfitting. Both teachers annotate the
test attempts, seeing the tutor's answer and correcting it, and reconcile disagreements; these gold labels are
what the baselines and the best configuration are compared against. The test set is opened once, after the
agentic optimization has finished, the best configuration is frozen and the evaluator is fixed by fingerprint.

\paragraph{Metrics and analysis} For the baselines and the best configuration the Study~1 metrics are computed
on the test set: phoneme-level precision, recall, F1 and diagnosis accuracy, verdict-level FAR and FRR, and
$\kappa$ with the gold labels. The difference between the best configuration and the baselines is tested with a
paired cluster bootstrap over learners: the difference is recomputed on the same resampled learners. If the best
configuration includes a fine-tuned model, it is also tested on a held-out set of expert recitation not used in
training: the upper bound of the 95\,\% interval of the PER difference must be below
one percentage point \cite{SRC-118}. The summary measure is the \emph{share of the gap closed}
\begin{equation}
  G_B = \frac{\kappa_B - \kappa_{B0}}{\kappa_{TT} - \kappa_{B0}},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_{TT}$ is the agreement between teachers: $G_B=0$ means the agentic optimization achieved nothing, and
$G_B=1$ means the system agrees with a teacher as well as teachers agree with each other. Always reported are
the \emph{log of the agentic optimization}, the \emph{learning curve} of the best configuration (25, 50, 75 and 100\,\% of training learners), the \emph{contribution of
data sources} --- teacher corrections only; plus the book's minimal pairs; plus learners' ``the bot was wrong''
disputes --- and the share of attempts the system decides on its own at a risk of at most $\varepsilon$, that
is, the workload it takes off the teacher. B1's risk--coverage curve is exploratory.

\paragraph{Fallback} If fewer than about 1\,500 annotated attempts exist in the training part by 15 April
2027, fine-tuning (B2) is removed from the components of the agentic optimization by a new preregistration version before
the test set is opened, and the agent searches for and combines configurations of off-the-shelf models without fine-tuning. The decision depends on the number of annotated
attempts, not on results.

\paragraph{Shadow mode} After the agentic optimization, the best configuration runs inside the tutor in shadow mode: the
learner sees B0's verdict, and its verdicts are stored in the attempt. Learners see the same system throughout
the pilot, so a model change does not confound their progress, and attempts after training give a
descriptive check.

\paragraph{Statistics} Holm's procedure controls the family-wise error
at $\alpha=0.05$ across the confirmatory hypotheses (their number is set once the hypotheses for RQ-2 are approved); effect sizes and confidence intervals are always
reported. Data collection stops at the planned end of the pilot; interim reports are descriptive only.

\section{Validity of development and experiments}\label{sec:method-harness}

The system is built by one author together with an AI agent, so its numbers must be protected from fitting not
only by the author but also by the agent. The development harness (Section~\ref{sec:impl-harness}) is described
as a means of validity and documented under goal G6 as an embedded case study \cite{SRC-102}: the case is the
development of the system; the units of analysis are working sessions and individual changes. There are four
data sources: the hook log, which records every block and approval request with time, hook and reason; session
summaries; the log of every request to the agent; and version history with ADRs. Metrics: blocks and approval
requests by reason; human interventions per session; iterations until checks pass; defects that passed all
checks; attempts by the agent to access the test set. Self-reported productivity is not used \cite{SRC-030}.
Whether this part becomes a separate research question or a separate paper is decided with the supervisor.

\section{Preregistration, provenance and reproducibility}\label{sec:method-prov}

Three mechanisms protect the results from being shaped after the fact.

\paragraph{Preregistration} Hypotheses, exclusions, sample size and analysis are written down before data
collection. When the author and supervisor approve the plan, its SHA-256 fingerprint and date are recorded,
and any later change requires a new version with a stated reason. Separately, before the agentic optimization first runs, the list of test learners and the RQ-1 and RQ-2 evaluator code are fixed
by fingerprint. Freezing data and
evaluator by hash follows the practice of the supervisor's group \cite{SRC-067}.

\paragraph{Protected evaluator and test set} The module that computes RQ-1 and RQ-2 metrics is a human-owned
zone: the coding agent must ask before changing it, because an agent that can edit the evaluator can
improve the score without improving the system \cite{SRC-027,SRC-082}. A hook forbids the agent to read the
test learners' labels: an agent that had seen the test set could fit a model to it.

\paragraph{Provenance} Every attempt stores the canonical variants, the recognised phonemes, the raw model
output, the thresholds, the code, curriculum and model versions, the book line as shown to the learner, and
the SHA-256 of the audio; a fine-tuned model carries the hash of its weights and of its training set. A
traceability command prints fingerprints of code, curriculum, SQL reports,
migrations, dependencies and preregistration. A number enters the thesis only if the corresponding analysis
is implemented and the figure or table has a passport: report, date, number of attempts and learners, and
code fingerprint.

\section{Ethics}\label{sec:method-ethics}

The study involves voice recordings on a religious topic, so it is designed as if the strictest reading of
the law applied. Consent to the service and consent to voice recording are separate; audio is never stored
without consent; consent can be withdrawn, which deletes recordings and their annotations; every learner can
delete all data with one button; only pseudonyms appear in reports and datasets; research participation is
limited to adults; no voice prints or speaker embeddings are computed; consent to voice recording
explicitly covers model training and the release of weights, and withdrawing consent removes the
recordings from subsequent versions of the dataset and the model; annotators see only the line and the
tutor's answer, never the learner's identity, and recordings are sent to them in protected mode
\cite{SRC-033,SRC-036,SRC-037}. The questions of who is the data operator, whether an ethics committee review
is required and where the server is located are institutional and are being settled with the supervisor
before learners outside the closed circle are invited.
